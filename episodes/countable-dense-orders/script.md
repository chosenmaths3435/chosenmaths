# What makes the rational numbers so special?

## s01 Hook
> visual: a number line with many dots at rationals, thicker near the middle. Label
> "the rational numbers".
[b1] Here are the rational numbers. Every fraction, positive and negative, sitting on a
line.

> visual: at "keep" every dot fades except those strictly between 0 and 1 and strictly
> between 2 and 3. Two clusters remain with an empty break between them. Hollow ticks at
> 0, 1, 2, 3.
[b2] Now throw most of them away. <bookmark mark="keep"/>Keep only the ones strictly
between zero and one, and the ones strictly between two and three.

> visual: the full line comes back. At "add" one gold dot drops in at sqrt(2), labelled.
[b3] Or keep them all, and <bookmark mark="add"/>add the square root of two, which isn't
a fraction.

> visual: the three lines stacked: all rationals, the two pieces, rationals plus sqrt(2).
> At "same" equals signs appear between them. Then the title card: "What makes the
> rational numbers so special?"
[b4] Those look like three different lines of dots. In one precise sense, they're
<bookmark mark="same"/>the same line. And the reason comes down to three facts about the
rationals.

## s02 A crowded line
> visual: the rationals line again. At "first" a gentle camera pan runs left, then right,
> with arrows on both ends. Tag at the corner: "1. a line, with no ends".
[b1] The first fact is the easy one. The rationals sit on a line, and
<bookmark mark="first"/>that line never ends. There's no first rational and no last one.

> visual: empty line. Two dots appear far apart, labelled a and b. At "avg" a smaller dot
> appears at their average. At "fill" smaller and smaller dots fill in between each
> neighboring pair, round after round, until the stretch is crowded with dots. Tag: "2.
> dense".
[b2] The second fact is that they're crowded. Take any two rationals. Their
<bookmark mark="avg"/>average is another rational between them. Now do that again
between those, <bookmark mark="fill"/>and again, and again. Between any two rationals sit
infinitely many more. The word for this is <bookmark mark="dense"/>dense.

## s03 Countable anyway
> visual: the line shows integers as large evenly spaced dots, with the rationals as a
> fine haze between each pair.
[b1] So you'd expect far more rationals than integers. The integers are spaced out, and
every stretch between them holds infinitely many rationals.

> visual: at "same" the haze pulses. Tag: "3. countable". Then a blank list appears at
> the top: an empty row of slots.
[b2] That's wrong, and it's the third fact. There are <bookmark mark="same"/>exactly as
many rationals as integers. The word is countable. It means you can write them all in one
list, with nothing left out.

> visual: a table builds. Row and column headers 1, -1, 2, -2, 3, -3, ... At "cell" the
> cells fill with fractions, row header over column header: 1/1, 1/-1, 1/2, ... on the
> first row, -1/1, -1/-1, ... on the second.
[b3] Here's how. Make a table. Along the top and down the side, write one, negative one,
two, negative two, and so on. <bookmark mark="cell"/>Each cell holds a fraction, the side
number over the top number.

> visual: a few cells flash, showing that a target fraction like 2/3 is in the table. At
> "twice" -1/-1 and 1/1 both flash as repeats.
[b4] Every rational except zero is in here somewhere. Many show up
<bookmark mark="twice"/>more than once, which won't matter.

> visual: "0" drops into the first slot of the list. A diagonal path runs through the
> cells, one antidiagonal at a time, from the top right of each diagonal to its bottom
> left. New values fly up into the list: 0, 1, -1, 1/2, 2, -1/2, -2, 1/3, 3, ... Repeats
> get crossed out and skipped. Speeds up.
[b5] Start the list with zero. Then <bookmark mark="walk"/>walk the diagonals one at a
time, skipping anything you've already seen. <bookmark mark="one"/>One. Negative one. One half. Two. Negative
one half. Negative two. Each diagonal ends, so you always reach the next one, and every
rational sits on some diagonal.

> visual: the table fades, the list stays on top. The line returns with the rationals of
> the list as dots. Each dot keeps its fraction label above it, and the integer spots are
> marked with their own labels below the line. At "fly" each dot in list order
> arcs to its spot: 0 to 0, 1 to 1, -1 to -1, 1/2 to 2, 2 to -2, -1/2 to 3, -2 to -3, 1/3
> to 4, 3 to -4. Faster and faster, more dots arriving, until the line is evenly spaced.
[b6] Now give each one an integer to sit on. <bookmark mark="fly"/>First on the list goes
to zero, next to one, next to negative one, then two, negative two, and so on. Every
rational gets its own integer, and every integer gets its own rational.

## s04 What got scrambled
> visual: freeze on the rearranged line, fraction labels above and spot labels below.
> Highlight the dot labelled 1/2 at spot 2, and the dot labelled 2 at spot -2. An arrow
> shows the 1/2 dot is on the right.
[b1] But look what that did to the order. <bookmark mark="half"/>One half now sits to the
right of two.

> visual: integers 3 and 4 with an empty bracket between them, next to two rationals with
> dots filling the space between them.
[b2] We matched up the dots, but we wrecked left and right. We had to. <bookmark mark="gap"/>Three and four are
neighbors, with nothing between them. No two rationals are
neighbors. Same count, different shape.

> visual: the three facts as a checklist: "a line, with no ends", "dense", "countable".
> A question mark beside a blank line of dots.
[b3] So here's the real question. Suppose another line of dots has all three facts. Does
it have to be the same shape as the rationals?

## s05 Disguises
> visual: the labels on the rationals fade away, leaving bare dots on a line.
[b1] Forget adding and multiplying. Forget the names of the numbers. Keep only left and
right.

> visual: two lines of a few dots, one above the other, the dots unevenly spaced. Threads
> connect pairs, none crossing. At "cross" one thread swings across another, flashes red,
> and returns.
[b2] We'll call two lines of dots the same if you can match them with threads. Every dot
on both lines gets exactly one thread, and <bookmark mark="cross"/>no two threads cross.
No crossing means left stays left.

> visual: the two-piece line from the start: rationals strictly between 0 and 1, and
> strictly between 2 and 3. Hollow ticks at 0, 1, 2, 3 to show they're left out. The
> three facts tick off one by one at "ends", "between" and "list". At "between" a dot a
> in the left piece and a dot b in the right piece light up, then a new dot appears
> between a and the hollow tick at 1.
[b3] Take that line with two pieces from the start. Zero and three are left out, so
<bookmark mark="ends"/>it has no first dot and no last dot. One and two are left out
too, so there's no last dot before the break. That means there's a dot between any two
dots, <bookmark mark="between"/>even across the break. And its dots are all rationals, so
<bookmark mark="list"/>you can list them too.

> visual: the two-piece line and the full rationals line, one above the other, with one
> bold "=" between them and the three facts beside it. Small on-screen tag: "order
> isomorphic".
[b4] So here's the claim. Any line with these three facts is the same as the rationals.
Every one of them is <bookmark mark="claim"/>the rationals in disguise. Let's prove it by
threading these two together.

## s06 The thread game
> visual: rationals on the top line, the two-piece line on the bottom. Beside the top line
> its list: 0, 1, -1, 1/2, 2, -1/2, ... Beside the bottom line its list: 1/2, 5/2, 1/3,
> 7/3, 2/3, 8/3, ... Numbers are shown again for this game. Top is blue, bottom is gold.
[b1] Put the rationals on top and the two-piece line on the bottom. Each line has
<bookmark mark="lists"/>a list, and <bookmark mark="switch"/>the bottom one switches between the pieces. We'll
draw one thread at a time. The two lines take turns, and I'll come back to why.
<bookmark mark="pick"/>On its turn, a line picks the first dot on its list without a thread.

> visual: 0 lights up on the top line. 1/2 lights up on the bottom. A thread joins them.
> Both list entries are crossed off. Every later thread crosses off an entry on each list.
[b2] Top goes first. Its first dot is zero. There are no threads yet, so any partner
works. We'll take <bookmark mark="t1"/>the first dot on the bottom list, one half.

> visual: 5/2 lights up on the bottom, to the right of the thread. The part of the top
> line right of 0 shades. The top list scans down and stops at 1. Thread.
[b3] Bottom's turn. It picks five halves, which is right of our thread. So its
partner must be right of zero. The first dot on the top list that fits is
<bookmark mark="t2"/>one.

> visual: -1 lights up on top, left of every thread. The part of the bottom line left of
> 1/2 shades. The bottom list scans and stops at 1/3. Thread.
[b4] Top's turn. It picks negative one, which is left of every thread. So its partner
must be left of one half. The first dot on the bottom list that fits is <bookmark mark="t3"/>one third.

> visual: 7/3 lights up on the bottom, between the threads from 1/2 and 5/2. The stretch
> of the top line between 0 and 1 shades. The top list scans and stops at 1/2. Thread.
[b5] Bottom's turn. It picks seven thirds, which falls between two threads, so its partner must fall
between those same two threads, somewhere from zero to one. The first dot that fits is
<bookmark mark="t4"/>one half.

> visual: pull back. Four threads, none crossing. Gaps between threads shade in matching
> pairs of colors on the two lines.
[b6] That's the whole game. A new dot falls in some gap between threads. Find a partner
in <bookmark mark="gaps"/>the matching gap on the other line. The new thread can't cross
anything.

## s07 Why you never get stuck
> visual: a new dot on the top line between two threads. The matching gap on the bottom
> line is shown enlarged, empty, with a question mark.
[b1] So the only way to lose is if the matching gap is empty.

> visual: the enlarged gap has a dot at each end. Dots fill in between them. Tag:
> "dense".
[b2] It never is. We've drawn finitely many threads, so the gap has
<bookmark mark="two"/>a dot at each end. And between any two dots, there's another.

> visual: a new dot far to the right of every thread on the top line. The bottom line
> extends beyond its last thread, and dots appear out there. Tag: "no ends".
[b3] If the new dot falls past all the threads, its partner has to be
<bookmark mark="out"/>farther out too. It's there, because the line has no last dot.

## s08 Back and forth
> visual: only the top list chooses for several rounds. On the bottom line one dot is
> ringed. Threads land on both sides of it, closer and closer, and it never gets one.
[b1] Now, why take turns? Suppose only the top line picks. Every top dot gets a partner.
But any dot in the matching gap is a fair partner, so we could
<bookmark mark="skip"/>miss this bottom dot forever.

> visual: the lists light up in alternation, top then bottom. Each list has a marker
> stepping down it. The ringed dot gets its turn and its thread.
[b2] Taking turns fixes that. <bookmark mark="turns"/>Every dot on the top line gets a
turn. Every dot on the bottom line gets a turn. They're lists, so no dot waits forever.

> visual: the game runs fast, threads zigzagging in order between the two lines, adding
> many threads per second, until the threads form a solid curtain with no crossings. The
> break in the bottom line stays empty, the threads fanning around it.
[b3] Keep going back and forth, <bookmark mark="fast"/>forever. In the end, every dot on
both lines has exactly one thread, and no two threads cross.

> visual: the curtain holds. Then the bottom line's piece labels fade, leaving the "=".
[b4] So the line with a break in it is the rationals in disguise. And we never used
anything about its two pieces. We only used the three facts, so this works for
<bookmark mark="any"/>any line that has them.

## s09 Ending
> visual: the three facts, each lit with its job beside it: "dense" fills the gaps, "no
> ends" gives room at the edges, "countable" gives every dot a turn.
[b1] Each fact had one job. Dense means <bookmark mark="d"/>the gaps are never empty. No
ends means <bookmark mark="e"/>there's always room at the edges. Countable means
<bookmark mark="c"/>every dot gets its turn.

> visual: the rationals line alone, with the three facts around it.
[b2] Those three facts pin down the order of the rationals completely. Anything else that
has them is the same line, under another name.

> visual: a solid real line. At "hole" one point is removed, leaving a ring.
[b3] The real numbers are dense with no ends too, but you can't list them, so the game
can't run. Take <bookmark mark="hole"/>one point out of the real line and you've made a
hole that wasn't there. That's a different line.

> visual: the dotted rationals line. One dot winks out. Nothing else changes.
[b4] Take <bookmark mark="out"/>one point out of the rationals, and you still have the
rationals.

## short1 The rationals in disguise
> visual: all rationals as dots on a line. Most fade, leaving two pieces, 0 to 1 and 2 to
> 3, with an empty break and hollow ticks at 0, 1, 2, 3. At "same" a large "=" appears
> between this line and a small copy of the full line.
[b1] Throw away almost every fraction. <bookmark mark="keep"/>Keep only the ones between zero and one, and the
ones between two and three. <bookmark mark="same"/>Nothing changed.

> visual: full rationals on top, the two pieces on the bottom. Threads appear and a bad
> crossing flashes red.
[b2] Put all the fractions on top and our two pieces below. We'll match dots with
threads, and <bookmark mark="cross"/>no two threads can cross. If every dot gets a thread,
left and right agree, so it's the same line.

> visual: both lists appear, and the game plays a few turns.
[b3] Both lines can be listed, so take turns. Pick the next dot on a list, and see which
gap between threads it falls in. <bookmark mark="match"/>Thread it to a dot in the
matching gap.

> visual: a matching gap fills with dots. Then a dot past the last thread gets a partner
> farther out.
[b4] That gap is never empty, because between any two dots on a line, there's another.
And past the last thread, there's always <bookmark mark="out"/>another dot, because
neither line has an end.

> visual: the game speeds up into a solid curtain of threads.
[b5] Take turns forever, and <bookmark mark="fast"/>every dot on both lines gets a thread.
A line with a break in it, matched perfectly to all the fractions.

## short2 As many fractions as integers
> visual: integers as big spaced dots with a haze of rationals between them. Large text:
> "#fractions = #integers".
[b1] There are exactly as many fractions as integers. That sounds wrong, because
<bookmark mark="haze"/>between any two integers sit infinitely many fractions.

> visual: the table builds with headers 1, -1, 2, -2, ...
[b2] But make a table. Along the top and down the side, write one, negative one, two,
negative two, and so on. <bookmark mark="cell"/>Each cell holds a fraction, the side
number over the top number. Every fraction except zero is in here.

> visual: the diagonal walk, with the list growing at the top: 0, 1, -1, 1/2, 2, ...
[b3] Start a list with zero. Then <bookmark mark="walk"/>walk the diagonals one at a
time, and skip repeats. Each diagonal ends, so you reach every fraction.

> visual: the rationals fly to integer spots in list order. They settle evenly spaced.
[b4] Now send the first one to zero, the next to one, the next to negative one, and on
<bookmark mark="fly"/>down the list.

> visual: full line of evenly spaced dots, each one labelled by a fraction.
[b5] Every fraction lands on its own integer, and every integer gets its own fraction.
That crowded line holds <bookmark mark="same"/>exactly as many dots as the spaced-out one.
