# An irrational to an irrational power

## short1 Irrational power
> visual: Open straight on the question, centered: "irrational ^ irrational = rational ?" with the two "irrational" words in one color and "rational" in another. On "yes" a green "Yes." appears under it. On "give" a small "...but which?" fades in below.
[b1] Can an irrational number raised to an irrational power be a rational number? <bookmark mark="yes"/>Yes. I can prove an example exists, but I can't <bookmark mark="give"/>give it to you.

> visual: Clear the question. Write "irrational = not a fraction of whole numbers" small at the top. On "root2" show the square root of two, large. On "tower" it gets an exponent of the square root of two, and a big "?" appears under the result.
[b2] Irrational means it's not a fraction of whole numbers, <bookmark mark="root2"/>like the square root of two. So take root two, <bookmark mark="tower"/>raised to the power root two. Is that rational? We don't need to know.

> visual: The tower moves to the top and a fork grows under it: two branches labelled "rational" (left) and "irrational" (right). On "yes1" the left branch ends in "a = root 2, b = root 2" with a green check.
[b3] <bookmark mark="fork"/>If it's rational, we're done. <bookmark mark="yes1"/>Both numbers are root two, and the answer is rational.

> visual: The right branch lights up. A copy of the tower slides down it, labelled "irrational" as the new base; on "again" it gets wrapped in brackets with one more exponent of root two. On "mult" the two exponents merge into one exponent "root 2 times root 2" over the base root 2, on "sq" that exponent becomes 2 (the whole expression now reads root 2 to the power 2, base highlighted), on "two" it collapses to a big "2". Then "a = root 2 ^ root 2, b = root 2" with a green check.
[b4] <bookmark mark="no"/>If it's irrational, it's a new irrational base. Raise it to the power root two <bookmark mark="again"/>once more. The exponents multiply, <bookmark mark="mult"/>and root two times root two is two. <bookmark mark="sq"/>So we're left with root two to the power two, <bookmark mark="two"/>which is just two. Rational.

> visual: Zoom out to the whole fork with both checks. On "which" a "which one?" label floats over the fork, between the two branches.
[b5] <bookmark mark="both"/>Either way, an example exists. But the proof never tells you <bookmark mark="which"/>which case actually happens.

> visual: On "answer" the left branch dims and crosses out, and "root 2 ^ root 2 is irrational" appears under the right one. On "year" a small line fades in: "Kuzmin 1930; in general, Gelfond-Schneider 1934". Hold to the end.
[b6] Mathematicians did find out later. It's the second case: <bookmark mark="answer"/>root two to the root two is irrational. But nobody could prove that until <bookmark mark="year"/>the nineteen thirties, and it's far harder than everything you just saw.
